      - >-
        The replacement railing was designed to mirror the railing on the opposite side. That brought balance to the stair opening and allowed a stronger visual connection between levels. The new work still needed to perform as part of the stair assembly, but visually it also had to look as though it belonged to the house rather than having been added after the fact.

    heading: Smooth ceilings throughout the house
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        Popcorn texture was removed in multiple areas so the ceilings could be prepared for a smooth finished appearance. That work involves more than taking texture off the surface. Existing ceiling conditions become visible after removal, and the substrate must be assessed, corrected where required, sanded under controlled conditions, primed and finished consistently.
      - >-
        The work was planned as dust-controlled, not literally dust-free. Containment, source extraction and cleanup helped limit migration, while the sequence protected completed flooring and finish carpentry.

  - type: text
    eyebrow: Work at height
    heading: The 20-foot ceiling required a different setup.
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        Standard floor-level access was not sufficient in the living room. The roughly 20-foot ceiling required scaffolding so the removal and finishing sequence could be carried out across the high, uninterrupted surface. Access, containment and equipment all had to work together at height while the room below remained protected.
      - >-
        A tall ceiling can magnify surface defects because daylight and interior lighting travel across a large field that is viewed from far below. Texture removal therefore could not be treated as the final step. Preparation and finishing still had to produce a consistent plane around the chandelier, pot lights and adjoining ceiling areas without inventing a different standard for the hard-to-reach section.

    heading: Small finish details determine whether the renovation looks complete.
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        Baseboards, corners, transitions and trim terminations are where several trades finally meet. A broad room view can look complete while poorly resolved edges still reveal the order in which disconnected jobs were carried out. Finish carpentry closes those gaps and gives the completed surfaces a common visual language.
      - >-
        The detail photograph shows the baseboard following the wall change above the hardwood. The value is not an oversized decorative gesture; it is the clean relationship between floor, wall, corner and trim. Those small decisions matter most where a new floor meets an existing plane or where demolition has changed the original wall geometry.
      - >-
        Careful painting and ceiling finishing then support the carpentry rather than masking it. Straight terminations, controlled joints and consistent surfaces allow the larger railing and ceiling work to feel settled into the home.

    eyebrow: Finished result
    heading: Different trades, one finished interior
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        The individual renovations were different trades, but the finished home needed to read as one project. Opening the half wall changed the staircase and the view between levels. The railing established a lighter architectural edge. Smooth ceilings quieted the surfaces above, while hardwood, tile and trim resolved the spaces underfoot and at the walls.
      - >-
        The result is not dependent on one showpiece. It comes from relationships: the new guard mirroring the existing railing, the flooring transition respecting both materials, the high ceiling reading consistently from below and the trim completing the edges around the renewed surfaces. Coordinating those relationships is what prevents a multi-trade renovation from looking pieced together.
