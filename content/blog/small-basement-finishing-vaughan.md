storySections:
  - eyebrow: The planning problem
    heading: A small footprint with a long list of requirements
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        Approximately 560 square feet can disappear quickly once stairs, mechanical equipment, circulation and enclosed rooms are accounted for. This Vaughan basement needed more than a basic open room. The plan had to include a shower, toilet, vanity, stackable washer and dryer, useful storage, finished stairs and a comfortable living area without making the basement feel like a series of narrow corridors.
      - >-
        Much of the footprint could easily have been lost to separate utility rooms and oversized passageways. The first task was therefore space planning, not finish selection. We identified the fixed elements, studied how people would move from the stairs into the basement and concentrated the service functions so the remaining floor area could stay open. That approach is central to thoughtful interior renovation planning when every square foot has more than one demand placed on it.

  - eyebrow: Planning stage
    heading: Exploring layouts in 3D
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        Drawings and 3D planning visualizations helped test the relationship between the bathroom, laundry, closet, doors and open living area before construction committed the space. More than one door arrangement was explored. That mattered because a door swing that appears minor on paper can interrupt circulation or reduce usable wall space in a compact basement.
      - >-
        The planning images also made the stacked laundry and bathroom layout easier to understand as a complete service block. A taped layout was then marked at the actual scale in the basement. Moving between plan, visualization and full-size layout helped confirm that the design could work in the real room rather than only on a screen.
  - eyebrow: Consolidated service block
    heading: Combining bathroom, laundry and closet functions
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        The bathroom contained the shower, toilet, vanity and stackable washer and dryer. Keeping the laundry within the same compact room avoided dedicating another enclosed room to a function that did not need to consume the living area. The bathroom and closet were placed back-to-back so plumbing, storage and circulation could be organized along one concentrated zone.
      - >-
        That arrangement gave the open area a clearer edge and reduced the number of small leftover spaces. The closet also created an additional buffer between the laundry area and the living space. It was a project-specific response to this footprint, not a claim of formal acoustic performance.

  - eyebrow: Structural coordination
    heading: Designing around structural posts
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        Structural posts could not simply be treated as inconvenient objects in the middle of the plan. Their locations helped determine where the bathroom walls and service block should sit. By incorporating the posts into those walls, the layout avoided awkward boxed columns in the open living area and made the enclosure feel intentional.
      - >-
        This is one reason small-basement planning must begin with an accurate existing-condition review. Structural elements, mechanical clearances and stair access all influence where rooms can realistically go. Working with those constraints preserved a cleaner open zone instead of forcing circulation around isolated obstructions.
  - eyebrow: Custom fixture
    heading: A bathroom vanity that also works as a utility sink
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        There was no room for a separate laundry or utility sink, but the basement still benefited from a basin that could handle occasional practical use. A custom vanity and custom countertop were built around an undermount sink so the same fixture could serve normal bathroom use and occasional utility tasks.
      - >-
        The solution kept the room visually consistent while avoiding another cabinet and another plumbing location. It was developed for the actual space available here; a dual-purpose vanity is not automatically appropriate for every bathroom or laundry plan.
  - eyebrow: Living-space priority
    heading: Preserving a large living area
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        Concentrating the bathroom, laundry, storage and structural conditions into one compact service block allowed the rest of the basement to remain substantially open. Instead of spending the floor area on multiple small rooms, the layout created one larger living zone with a direct route from the staircase and useful wall lengths for furniture.
      - >-
        The result feels larger than the measurement suggests because circulation is shared with the living area rather than carved out as a separate hallway wherever possible. Storage was planned alongside that circulation, so doors could open without blocking the primary path through the basement.
  - eyebrow: Window detailing
    heading: Making small basement windows feel larger
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        The basement windows remained physically small, but oversized trim and framing gave the openings more architectural presence. The wider treatment helped each window read as a deliberate finished feature rather than a minor opening left over from the original utility space.
      - >-
        This detail did not increase the amount of daylight entering the basement. Its value was visual: stronger proportions made the openings feel less insignificant within the completed walls and connected them to the surrounding trim and finish work.
  - eyebrow: Stair transition
    heading: Oak staircase and railing
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        The stairs form the transition from the main floor into the basement, so leaving them as a utility stair would have weakened the finished result. Solid red-oak tread caps and a solid red-oak railing gave the stair a durable, coordinated finish and established that the basement was part of the home's living space.

      - >-

      - >-
        A small footprint does not make every major component proportionally cheaper. Bathrooms, plumbing, electrical work, stairs, cabinetry and custom finishing carry many of the same fixed costs whether the basement is 560 or 1,000 square feet.
