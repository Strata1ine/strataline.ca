homeownerQuestions:
  - question: Can an existing staircase be refinished without replacing the structure?
    answer: This project is documented as refinishing, and the photographs show the same established flight and landing geometry before and after. Another staircase still needs an on-site condition assessment; the photos do not establish concealed structural work.
  - question: When should the railing be replaced during stair refinishing?
    answer: Replacement is worth considering when the existing railing conflicts with the intended stair finish or when posts, handrail and balusters need to be redesigned together. This Etobicoke project coordinated both visible scopes at the same time.
  - question: How should stair stain coordinate with surrounding hardwood flooring?
    answer: Exact matching is not always visually necessary, but the relationship should be deliberate at landings, nosings and post bases. Here, repeated dark stair and railing elements are separated cleanly from white risers and stringers.
  - question: Stair refinishing or stair capping—which fits an existing staircase?
    answer: This project was documented as stair refinishing, not capping. The appropriate method depends on the existing tread condition, geometry and desired result, which must be assessed directly rather than inferred from these photographs.
homeownerInsights:
  - Plan tread, riser, stringer, landing and railing finishes as one visible composition.
  - Compare the staircase against adjacent flooring from both the upper and lower levels.
