    heading: The curves were already striking. The finishes made them feel dated.
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        Before the renovation, the Kleinburg staircase was finished almost entirely in one honey-toned wood colour. Treads, risers, handrails, posts and closely spaced wood balusters shared a similar tone. The sweeping geometry was visible, but the individual lines of the staircase blended together instead of being clearly defined.
      - >-
        The before photographs also show how many connected surfaces had to be considered. A curved lower flight rises into winders, passes an open guard and continues toward an upper landing that was carpeted. A second run and several railing transitions remain visible from the main floor. Changing one surface in isolation would have left the staircase visually fragmented.
      - >-
        The renovation kept the focus on that existing geometry while changing how each horizontal, vertical and curved element reads. The photographs establish a comprehensive visible finish transformation; they do not establish the concealed construction method, wood species or product system, so those details are deliberately not claimed here.

    heading: Why the piano-key effect works
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        The nickname makes sense as soon as you see the finished staircase: dark treads and crisp white risers create the same alternating rhythm as piano keys. It is a descriptive visual comparison, not a theme applied on top of the architecture. Every step repeats the dark horizontal and light vertical relationship, so the effect becomes stronger as the staircase rises and turns.
      - >-
        Contrast also gives the complicated geometry a useful hierarchy. Dark treads trace the route underfoot. White risers and stringers separate each level and connect with the surrounding trim. Dark handrails and posts outline the open edges, while the lighter vertical railing elements keep the guards visually connected to the white stair surfaces.

    heading: The repeated stair profile carries the transformation.
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        On a staircase this open, the tread-and-riser relationship is visible from many directions. Looking upward from the main floor, the white risers form a continuous sequence beneath the dark nosings. Looking across the landing, the dark tread edges relate directly to the adjacent dark flooring. That consistency is what turns an individual colour choice into an architectural pattern.
      - >-
        The curved portions and winders are especially important. Their edges change direction as the stair turns, so the dark nosing line must remain visually controlled through shapes that are not simple repeated rectangles. White risers make those intersections more exposed: irregular edges would stand out immediately against the sharp contrast.

The dark wood outlines the movement; the lighter areas keep the open guards bright; and the vertical elements reinforce the cadence established by the steps. The photographs support a coordinated change in colour and visible railing details, but not a claim that every concealed or reusable component was replaced.

    heading: Curves, winders and transitions leave little room for visual inconsistency.
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        The most demanding parts are visible where several lines meet: a tread nosing turns into a curved stringer, a post lands beside a change in floor level, or a handrail changes direction around an open landing. The after photographs show these intersections from above, below and across the room, which is why alignment matters throughout the project rather than only in one front-facing view.
      - >-
        White stringers and risers also make finish boundaries easy to inspect. The dark tread edges need to terminate cleanly, vertical faces need consistent coverage, and railing lines need to follow the curve without looking interrupted. These are observable finish qualities; the photographs do not provide measurements or hidden assembly details, so none are inferred.

    eyebrow: Finish coordination
    heading: The staircase now relates to the dark flooring around it.
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        A staircase is read beside the floors that meet it. In the finished views, the dark tread surfaces and nosings share a close visual relationship with the dark floor at the landing. The white risers, stringers and surrounding trim form a bright boundary between those horizontal wood surfaces, preventing the result from becoming visually heavy.
      - >-
        The colour relationship remains intentional under different viewing angles. From below, the white faces dominate and emphasize the ascent. From the landing, the dark horizontal surfaces align with the floor. From the side, the handrail and posts provide a dark outline against the pale walls. That balance is more important than making every material appear identical in every light.
  - type: text
    eyebrow: Finished result
    heading: The completed staircase has a stronger architectural presence.
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        The renovation did not need to disguise the staircase’s traditional curves. Instead, the dark-and-white finish gives those curves greater definition. Treads form a bold repeated path, white risers sharpen every step, and coordinated railing contrasts carry the composition upward through the open landings.
      - >-
        The result earns the “piano stairs” description because the visual rhythm is genuine and consistent. It is still a professional, restrained interior finish: the effect comes from repeated geometry and a limited palette rather than decorative novelty.

    eyebrow: Homeowner takeaways
    heading: What this Kleinburg project shows
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        A strong staircase design begins with the whole visible system. Treads, risers, stringers, guards, posts, handrails and nearby floors all affect whether the finished work reads as one renovation.
    listHeading: Planning lessons from the project
    listItems:
      - High contrast can clarify a curved staircase’s geometry without changing its traditional character.
      - Dark treads and white risers are most effective when the treatment remains consistent through landings and winders.
      - Railing colours and vertical details should be planned with the stair surfaces, not selected afterward in isolation.
      - White risers and stringers make tread edges and finish intersections especially visible.
      - Adjacent flooring should be considered when choosing the visual weight and colour of stair surfaces.
      - Before-and-after photographs from matching viewpoints make the effect of finish coordination easier to evaluate.
