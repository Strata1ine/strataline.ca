challenge: The original curves had to remain, but the carpet, wood spindles, uneven railing ends, leading step and surrounding floor needed to be resolved as one coordinated renovation.
scope:
  - Remove the carpet and assess the existing stair structure.
  - Fit red-oak tread caps through the curved flights and landings.
  - Finish the risers white to clarify the stair profile.
  - Replace wooden spindles with black metal balusters.
  - Coordinate baluster spacing through the curves and upper guards.
  - Make a matching curved railing termination by hand.
  - Correct the leading step with matching bullnose returns.
  - Replace approximately 600 square feet of hardwood with large-format 2-by-2-foot marble tile.
solution: The stair, railing, landings and floor were planned together so the original curved architecture could remain while every visible finish and termination was brought into the same design language.
result: The completed entrance reads as one continuous multi-level staircase, with stronger contrast, clearer sightlines and railing details that support rather than compete with the original curves.

      - >-
        This multi-level curved staircase was the architectural centre of the home, but carpeted treads, wood spindles, mismatched railing ends and the surrounding hardwood floor made the entrance feel dated and disconnected. The renovation had to preserve the original curves while coordinating the treads, railings, leading step, landings and new large-format floor as one finished interior.
      - >-
        We removed the carpet, assessed the existing stair structure, fitted red-oak caps, finished the risers white and replaced the wooden spindles with black metal balusters. The railing work continued through the upper guards rather than stopping at the main flight. We also corrected the leading step, made a corresponding railing curl by hand and coordinated the stair finishes with approximately 600 square feet of new marble flooring. Each decision had to support the same sightlines because the staircase is visible from the entrance, upper hall and adjoining rooms.

  - eyebrow: Curved staircase planning
    heading: Why the curved geometry mattered
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        A curved staircase cannot be treated as a row of isolated steps. The tread shapes establish a sweep that must continue through the nosing lines, handrail, baluster layout and upper guards. If one part changes direction or spacing without reference to the others, the inconsistency is visible from several angles. On this project, we kept checking the work from the entrance and both landings, not only from the step being fitted.


  - eyebrow: Existing conditions
    heading: The original staircase
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        The original staircase combined light carpet on the walking surfaces with honey-toned wood spindles, posts and rails. The carpet interrupted the hardwood floor at the base and made the main flight read separately from the upper landings. The closely spaced wood spindles added visual weight to an already complex multi-level form, especially where several guards overlapped in the same view.


  - eyebrow: Stair preparation
    heading: Removing the carpet and capping the stairs
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        Once the carpet was removed, the existing stair had to be evaluated before any cap was fitted. Tread caps depend on a sound, suitable structure beneath them; they are not automatically the right approach for every staircase. Here, the existing construction could support the planned system after preparation, allowing the original curved geometry to remain instead of rebuilding the entire stair.
      - >-
        Red oak was selected because it could be fitted to the visible tread shapes and finished in relation to the retained woodwork. Each cap had to follow the curve, maintain a consistent nosing line and meet the riser cleanly. Small dimensional changes from step to step mattered because an uneven projection would become obvious when viewed along the full flight.

  - eyebrow: Railing coordination
    heading: Rebuilding the visual rhythm of the railing
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        Replacing the wood spindles with black metal balusters changed more than the material. The slimmer profiles opened the views through the staircase and reduced the visual mass where multiple flights and guards overlap. We coordinated their spacing through the curved sections and landings so the pattern did not tighten abruptly, drift or appear to restart at every post.
      - >-
        Existing handrail and post elements were retained or modified only where they remained suitable for the finished system. The goal was not to replace sound wood simply for the sake of replacement; it was to make the main stair flight and upper guards read as one railing composition. The decorative metal details were repeated with restraint so the curves kept their character without returning to the heaviness of the original wood spindle layout.

  - eyebrow: Custom craftsmanship
    heading: The handmade railing curl
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        The key craftsmanship detail was the curved termination at the front of the railing. The two existing ends met their newel posts differently. On the left, the post extended beyond the handrail. On the right, the post was mounted below the curled end. The homeowner wanted the visible curves to relate even though the supporting conditions were not identical.
      - >-
        We made the corresponding curved termination by hand, shaping it to follow the sweep and visual proportion of the opposite side. It did not need to disguise the different post construction. It needed to make the two ends feel intentional when seen together from the entrance. That measured correction is a small part of the material list, but it has a large effect on whether the completed staircase looks coordinated.
  - eyebrow: Entrance detail
    heading: Correcting the leading step
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        The first step is wider and more exposed than the steps above it, so its edge treatment establishes the stair before a person begins to climb. Originally, one side had a rounded bullnose return while the other side ended differently. The imbalance was visible beneath the paired newel posts and became more noticeable once the carpet and wood spindles were removed.
      - >-
        We gave the opposite end a matching bullnose treatment. The correction balanced the leading step, aligned it with the railing curls above and created a cleaner transition from the surrounding floor. It is a useful reminder that stair modernization is not only a choice of stain or spindle. Returns, nosings and rail ends are the details that make the larger material changes look complete.
  - eyebrow: Finish coordination
    heading: Coordinating the staircase with the surrounding floor
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        The project extended beyond the stair because the entrance floor strongly affected how the new work would read. Approximately 600 square feet of hardwood was replaced with large-format 2-by-2-foot marble tile. The lighter floor created contrast beneath the dark stair components and continued across the landings, helping the multi-level composition feel connected rather than divided into separate finishes.

homeownerInsights:
  - Curved staircases can often be modernized without changing their fundamental geometry.
  - Tread caps depend on the condition and suitability of the existing stair structure.
  - Railing work should include the stair flight and upper guards in one coordinated plan.
  - Small details such as railing ends and leading-step returns affect whether the result feels complete.
  - Stain, paint, metalwork and adjacent flooring should be selected together.
  - A staircase quote should identify exactly what remains, what is replaced and what is refinished.
