A person entering had to negotiate the door, toilet and tub edge before using any fixture. The renovation therefore began with the floor plan rather than a simple exchange of old finishes for new ones.

      - >-

homeownerQuestions:
  - question: How can a pocket door improve a small condo bathroom?
    answer: It removes the clearance required by a hinged slab. In this King West bathroom, the door withdraws into the wall and stops competing with the toilet, vanity and shower approach; another room still requires a suitable wall cavity and utility review.
  - question: Can shower controls be relocated during renovation?
    answer: They often can when the plumbing route and wall conditions permit. Here, the control was moved near the shower entrance so the client can turn on and adjust the water before stepping fully inside.
  - question: Why does bathroom demolition require advance condo planning?
    answer: Equipment, protection and debris movement must follow building access rules. Reserving the service elevator and demolition window lets the crew bring up the required equipment and remove debris during the approved period.
  - question: What documents may a condo corporation require from a contractor?
    answer: Requirements vary, but corporations commonly request renovation forms, insurance documents and WSIB information where applicable, along with details about common-area protection and building access.
  - question: Why must service-elevator reservations be planned carefully?
    answer: Another resident or contractor may have the next booking. Tools, equipment, debris carts and later material deliveries therefore need to be staged around the reserved access instead of moved whenever convenient.
  - question: How does a floating vanity affect a small bathroom visually?
    answer: It exposes more visible floor and reduces the amount of cabinetry meeting the floor. In this project, that helps the central route appear clearer beside the modern toilet and glass shower.
  - question: Should a bathroom layout be finalized before plumbing and electrical rough-ins?
    answer: Yes. This project required the shower, control, pocket door, vanity, toilet and pot-light positions to be coordinated while the walls were open, before finishes concealed the connected work.
homeownerInsights:
  - Confirm the condo corporation's current renovation and service-elevator process before scheduling demolition.
  - Stage equipment, debris removal and later material deliveries around the reserved access window.
  - Finalize the door, shower entry and control positions before plumbing and electrical rough-ins begin.
  - Review wall structure and utilities before assuming a pocket door will fit another bathroom.
  - Treat vanity, toilet, glass, lighting and tile as parts of one compact visual layout.
