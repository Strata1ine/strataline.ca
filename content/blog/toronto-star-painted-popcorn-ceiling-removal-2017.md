    heading: Why painted popcorn required a different approach
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        In 2017, Strataline worked in a 1,200-square-foot Mississauga condo owned by Toronto sound editor Mark Dejczak. The Toronto Star reported that approximately 750 square feet of ceiling required work and that the family remained in the condo during the renovation. Cleanliness therefore formed part of the technical scope rather than being left until final cleanup.
      - >-
        The ceiling texture had been painted. A coating can reduce the ability of water to penetrate the texture, changing how readily it softens for scraping. Repeated wetting and hand scraping can become slow, create sticky debris and leave an uneven substrate requiring further correction. The method had to address the coating while protecting the occupied rooms and daily access routes.

  - type: text
    eyebrow: The technical process
    heading: How the vacuum-connected system worked
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        The process combined power sanding with a vacuum connection at the tool, source extraction, plastic containment, an appropriate abrasive and controlled sanding technique. On this project, a Festool Planex was connected to a Festool dust extractor. Capturing material where the abrasive met the ceiling reduced the amount available to travel through the condo.
      - >-
        Mechanical removal was not the finish line. The exposed ceiling was inspected, corrected and skim coated where required before primer and paint. Pot-light locations were coordinated before the final finish so completed surfaces would not be reopened later.
      - >-
        The Toronto Star reported that Strataline had invested approximately $5,000 in specialized sanding and extraction equipment for this work in 2017. The equipment supported dust control; containment, technique and disciplined cleanup completed the system. Dust-controlled is the accurate description, not a claim of literal zero airborne particles.
